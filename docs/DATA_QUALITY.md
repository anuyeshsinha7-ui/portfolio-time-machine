## How the prices were rebuilt

1. **Yahoo raw prices.** Yahoo's `Close` (downloaded with `auto_adjust=False`) is already divided by every split Yahoo knows about. We undo that with Yahoo's own split history to get Yahoo's view of the exchange price.
2. **Anchored to NSE.** On the first trading day of every month we compare that price with NSE's official close in the bhavcopy. Wherever the ratio steps to a new level, we bisect day by day to the exact date and rescale. **43 stocks** had **59** such hidden level errors in Yahoo — splits recorded with the wrong ratio, bonuses never recorded, or demergers Yahoo adjusted silently (e.g. Reliance × 0.923 before 20 Jul 2023).
3. **Our own adjustments.** Every split, bonus, rights issue and demerger is re-applied from NSE's corporate-action records (`data/corporate_actions.csv`, one row per action with its source), confirmed by the price move on the ex-date.
4. **Dividends** come from NSE's records (₹ per share, or % of face value for older notices) and are reinvested on the ex-date (price before ex-date × (1 − dividend ÷ previous close)). Result: a total-return price series.
5. **Checks** for decimal errors, spike-and-revert bad ticks (verified against NSE before removal), stale prices, gaps and calendar alignment.

## Benchmark caveat

Stock prices here include reinvested dividends, but the Nifty 50 (`^NSEI`) is a *price* index. Beta and every 'versus Nifty 50' comparison therefore slightly favour the stocks (by roughly the index dividend yield, about 1–1.5% a year).

## Residual issues to keep in mind

* Six Nifty 50 sessions before 2012 are missing from Yahoo and NSE's archive has no index file for them; they are left out of the calendar, so the stock returns of those days merge into the next session.
* Older demergers (before NSE's 2023 special pre-open methodology) have their ex-date return dropped rather than adjusted.
* Rights issues use the theoretical ex-rights price; partly-paid issues and warrants are not adjusted.
